# Player afterburner

Branch: `main` (work-unit commits)
Delivery: `ask-on-risk` default, one work unit (about 300 authored lines)
Test runner: `./run.sh --headless tests/smoke_test.tscn`

## Specs

**S1.** The ship has two thrust levels. Normal thrust keeps `MAX_SPEED = 26.8`
and the existing acceleration. The afterburner targets `BOOST_SPEED = 42`
(about 1.57x) with a stronger `BOOST_ACCELERATION`, and only engages while there
is movement input: holding the burner while hovering must not spend reserve.

**S2.** The afterburner runs on a turbo reserve of `TURBO_MAX = 100`. Burning
drains 45/s (about 2.2 s of continuous burn from full); not burning recharges
24/s (about 4.2 s from empty), including while the ship hovers, because "normal
speed" includes standing still. At zero the burn cuts off and the ship falls
back to `MAX_SPEED`; it re-arms only when the player releases the boost input, so
holding the button at empty cannot stutter the ship between two speeds.

**S3.** The thruster is visible. Twin flames sit behind the hull and scale with
the forward component of the ship's velocity (so strafing with the nose
elsewhere gives a short flame instead of a lie), turn hotter and longer while
burning, and the camera gets a small FOV kick as a speed cue.

**S4.** The HUD shows the reserve as a continuous bar above the segmented HP bar.
The player publishes `GlobalEventBus.player_turbo_changed(current, maximum)` when
the rounded reserve changes, so the bus does not carry a per-frame stream.

**S5.** Controls: Shift / right trigger is the afterburner; Space / left shoulder
stays the dash (a 0.16 s invulnerable burst, a different tool). The input map is
written through `tools/setup_input_map.gd`, and the on-screen hint lists both.

**S6.** Checks: the smoke test proves the burn exceeds `MAX_SPEED`, the reserve
drains while burning, refills while coasting, and drops the ship back to normal
speed when it burns out. Docs and the feature log record the numbers.

## Log

**L1.** User request, verbatim:

> ahora quiero que trabajemos en el comportamiento de la nave player, toma en cuenta lo siguiente:
> 1. el avion tiene propulsores (como un jet) el cual tiene una velocidad normal y otra raplida.
> 2. al usar la velocidad rapida se consume una barra turbo la cual se recarga al ir a velocidad normal.

**L2.** Design notes.

- The afterburner is a sustained speed mode, so it is not the existing dash:
  the dash is a 0.16 s burst that overrides velocity and grants i-frames. Both
  stay; they answer different questions (cross ground / dodge a pattern).
- Boost is gated on movement input so the reserve measures distance covered,
  not button time.
- Burning out is a state, not a value: it clears on release only. The
  alternative (re-arm at a partial refill while the button is held) produces a
  visible stutter cycle around the refill threshold.
- Speed is the only thing the afterburner changes. Turning stays on the same
  acceleration curve, so boosting does not make the ship harder to steer.

**L3.** Evidence.

- `./run.sh --headless tests/smoke_test.tscn` — 35 assertions, all pass:
  - turbo starts full;
  - afterburner reaches 42.0 u/s against a 26.8 normal top speed;
  - reserve 100 -> 32 after 1.5 s of burn (45/s) and 32 -> 56 after 1.0 s off
    the burner (24/s);
  - burnout drops the ship back to 26.8 u/s while the button stays held;
  - releasing the button and refilling re-arms the burner (42.0 u/s again);
  - turbo changes reach `GlobalEventBus`;
  - the FOV kick lands (58.0 -> 63.0).
- Visual: `tools/capture_frame.tscn -- --boost 30` holds move_right and the
  burner and captures mid-burn. Idle shows two small nozzle glows; burning
  shows two ~2.8-unit plumes with bright cores behind the hull, the reserve bar
  partially spent above the HP bar.
- Flame scale note: the ship is only about 28 px wide on screen, so the plume is
  sized against that budget (2.8 units of length), not against the hull radius.
  The first pass was a single cone and read as an opaque white arrow.
